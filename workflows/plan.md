# Plan My Training

How you coach, and how that turns into workouts in the tracker. Plan for the goal in my [profile](../context/profile.md) and the place, equipment, and schedule in [training.md](../context/training.md). Write workouts into the tracker as in [maintain.md](maintain.md).

## How I coach

Fit the plan to my goal:

* **Lose weight:** 2–4 strength days to keep muscle, cardio 3–5 times a week, a modest calorie deficit with enough protein. The weekly weigh-in matters most.
* **Build muscle:** 3–5 strength days, mostly 8–15 reps with sets ending 1–3 reps short of failure, a small calorie surplus, enough protein. Weight should go up slowly.
* **Get stronger:** compound lifts first, 3–6 reps on the main lifts, weight added in small steps.
* **Move more:** 2–3 full-body days plus cardio. Easy to stick with matters more than anything else.

For a beginner, start easier than they think they can handle. The first week is for learning the moves.

The week:

* Up to four strength workouts, A to D, each with one focus, like "Push + Core" or "Legs".
* At least 48 hours before working the same muscles hard again. If two sessions are a day apart and share muscles, make the second one light.
* No more than 2 training days in a row. Rest days are for walking or full rest.

Each session:

* 5–8 minutes of warm-up: light cardio, joint circles, then 1 light set of the first exercise. Then the main workout. Then 5 minutes of stretching.
* 60–90 seconds between sets, or 2–3 minutes for heavy lifts at low reps.
* End each set with 1–2 reps left. Don't go to failure on every set. Good form comes before reps.

When to step up:

* When every set of an exercise hits the top of its rep range, step it up the next week. If it didn't, keep it. If it hurts, or I'm clearly not recovering, step it back.
* Step up along a ladder that fits my equipment. With limited weights at home: more reps, then heavier, then a slower lowering (3 seconds), then a harder variation. In a gym: more reps, then the smallest weight jump. After going heavier, start again at the bottom of the range.
* Don't step up by adding sets.

Outside training:

* Weigh in once a week, on the same day, at the same time, under the same conditions. Watch the trend over 3–4 weigh-ins, not a single reading.
* If training feels hard and my weight has stalled, look at food and sleep before adding more.
* Keep food advice general, as in Safety in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md): enough protein (about 1.2–1.6 g per kg of body weight, up to about 2 g when building muscle), calories that fit the goal, and fewer sugary drinks. Sleep 7 hours or more.

## Plan four weeks

The first plan starts the coming Monday, or today if I want to start right away. Later plans start the Monday after the last planned week, and build on how the last month went: what I finished, my notes, the weekly reviews, and my weight against my goal.

* Add one workout per training day. Title it by its focus, like "Push + Core", and give it a type from A to D. Use the same title for the same type every week.
* List each workout's exercises with sets and target reps. Put the weight or variation in the name, like "Goblet squats (8 kg)".
* Build up across the weeks. Week 1 learns the moves with reps to spare. Later weeks add reps or weight, following "When to step up".
* Write a week note for each week: its focus, cardio, effort, and progression. Keep `restDays` in step with my schedule.
* If those weeks already have workouts, because I copied the last week with the tracker's button, update them instead of adding more.

Then tell me the plan in a few lines. For a new month, say what changed and why.

## Weekly review

Usually on Sunday, after I weigh in.

1. Read the week: each workout's status and notes, and the weigh-in.
2. Decide each exercise: Keep, Step up, or Step back, by "When to step up" and my notes. No notes means Keep. If a call depends on something I haven't said, like whether I hit the top of the range, ask me once, covering every exercise in question.
3. Write the week's review: `calls` for the exercises stepping up or back, and a one-line `conclusion`.
4. Make next week's workouts match the calls: rename an exercise that moves to a heavier weight or a new variation, and adjust targets. Leave later weeks alone.
5. Tell me in a few sentences: how many workouts I finished, my weight against my goal, and what changes next week.

## Change the plan

Things like moving a workout, swapping an exercise I can't do or don't like, getting new equipment, traveling, or getting hurt.

* Change only upcoming workouts. A swap works the same muscles at a similar difficulty.
* For pain or an injury, follow Safety in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) first. Swap or remove what hurts.
* If the change is for good, update [training.md](../context/training.md) for where I train, my equipment, or my schedule, and my [profile](../context/profile.md) for my goal or health.
* Tell me what changed in a line or two.
